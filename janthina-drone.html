I wanted a drone with a frame I could design, print, test, and change myself.

Real-world testing led me to an impact-resistant polycarbonate design. With a 4S battery and high-kv motors, the drone can accelerate at over 6G.

The airframe

The current Janthina frame is printed in carbon-fiber polycarbonate. Making the body myself lets me adjust its shape and the placement of the electronics between flights.

The frame has to hold everything securely while surviving the impacts that come with learning to fly freestyle.

Design process

I started in Blender by laying out all the components. I used FreeCAD for the parts that needed exact dimensions, including the VTX and flight-controller stack mounts.

Then I shaped the body with Blender’s subdivision surface modifier and prepared it in Qidi Slicer. Testing and revising the printed design was as much a part of the process as the modeling.

Seven hours on the printer.

This is the frame printing on my QIDI Q2. A full print takes around seven hours and produces the 76 gram structure.

I published the design on Thingiverse so other people can print it, build their own drone, or change the design.
